The Challenge of Image Generation

Why is it Difficult?

Historically, generating realistic images required complex manual modeling. Generative AI shifted the paradigm toward data distribution learning.

  • Computational Realism: Capturing textures and global structures simultaneously.
  • Role of the Survey: Organizing the "profusion" of models published in recent years.
  • Objective: Providing a guide for beginners and professionals on the state of the art.

Fundamentals of GANs

The Generator (G)

The "Forger". Tries to create synthetic samples from random noise that are indistinguishable from real data.

The Discriminator (D)

The "Detective". Learns to distinguish between real images from the database and fake ones created by the Generator.

Adversarial Process

Constant competition where both evolve. The Generator seeks to deceive, and the Discriminator seeks to detect.

From DCGAN to Modern Models

  • 2014: First GANs
    Proposed by Ian Goodfellow. Blurry, low-resolution samples.
  • DCGAN
    Introduction of Convolutional Networks, stabilizing training and enabling larger scale.
  • StyleGAN & Progressive GAN
    Ability to generate photorealistic images in high resolution (1024x1024).

Where are GANs Applied?

Image Translation

Domain conversion (e.g., map to aerial photo, black and white to colorized).

Super-Resolution

Restoration of old photos and resolution enhancement while maintaining sharp details.

Synthetic Data

Generation of datasets to train other AIs where real data is scarce.

What AI has not yet solved

Mode Collapse

The generator "gets stuck" and starts producing repetitive or nearly identical images to deceive the discriminator.

Other Challenges:

  • High Computational Cost (days of training).
  • Evaluation Difficulty (metrics like FID are not always perfect).
